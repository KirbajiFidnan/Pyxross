# Decision Log

> **Status:** D01–D63 locked during the design phase + Godot implementation (2026-09-06 … 2026-09-08). **D64 (2026-09-10) supersedes the implementation stack: Godot/GDScript is abandoned — the product design decisions (D01–D63) remain binding unless explicitly superseded; the *implementation* decisions (D02, D22, D29, D30, D42) are re-expressed for Rust.** D65–D67 add the Projection System and window integration. **D68–D74 (2026-09-25) rework the selection system into a single flat `Fieldier` tool; D74 supersedes D68's child modes, D71 and D73's wand clauses, and they narrow D32 while adding pixel operations alongside D50/D54 rather than replacing them.** **D75 (2026-09-26) reinstates the Fieldier children and supersedes D74.** **D76 (2026-09-26) refines the Fieldier selection's appearance, area-move and gestures, superseding the named clauses of D50, D72 and D75 and narrowing D32.** **D77 (2026-09-26) fixes the Fieldier's area-move, combining, right-button and Alt-sweep semantics, superseding the named clauses of D71, D72, D75 and D76 and re-affirming D50 and D70.** **D78 (2026-09-26) locks each selection gesture's combining mode at press, makes every Fieldier child subtract symmetrically on the right button, and applies the Wand shape once per click with modifier-aware double-click combining, superseding the named clauses of D77 and re-affirming D50, D70 and D32.** **D79 (2026-09-26) rebinds the Fieldier selection flips and adds a rotate: Shift+F flips vertically, Ctrl+F flips horizontally, Alt+F rotates 90° CW around the selection's bounding-box center, each one undo step affecting both the mask and the pixels inside it (superseding D72's flip clause).** Milestone numbers R0… replace the old M0… series (see ROADMAP.md).
> **Convention:** New decisions get the next free number. A decision can only be changed by adding a new decision that supersedes it (history is never deleted).

| **D76** | **Fieldier selection refinements (2026-09-26).** Six refinements to the Fieldier's selection appearance, area-move and gestures on top of D75. **(1) One selection appearance.** The committed selection is ALWAYS rendered as the mask marching-ants (`CanvasOverlay::AntsMask` over `Selection::outline_segments`), including when the mask happens to be a rectangle; the dedicated rectangular `CanvasOverlay::Selection` path is only a fallback/legacy path and the rectangle is no longer special-cased for display. Rationale: a rectangle and a T-shape must not look like two different features. **(2) Mask survives a move.** Dragging the selection area (`AreaMove`) keeps the mask's notches/protrusions visible during the drag (the live preview is the translated mask boundary, not a filled bounding box), and committing the move translates the mask itself (`move_selection_to_command`, previously rectangular-only, is extended to mask selections). This supersedes D50's rectangular-only move commit and D75's `AreaMove` presentation. **(3) Calmer marching ants.** A fixed, non-animated mid-grey solid under-stroke (`marching_ants_under`, `ANT_UNDER_STROKE_ALPHA`) is painted beneath the white dashed ants, the dash/gap is shortened to 3 px, and the drift speed is reduced to 0.003 px/ms (one 6 px period ≈ 2 s). Brightness still never changes. **(4) Inverted live box.** The live selection-drag outline takes its color from the inverse (`255 - channel`) of the canvas pixel under the pointer (falling back to the theme stroke when the pointer is off-canvas), so a white/near-white canvas still shows a visible box. **(5) No Alt+click-drag lift.** Alt+left-click inside a committed selection no longer lifts a floating transform (the "copy" lift is removed). **Ctrl+click inside a selection still lifts it (D32 intact)**, as a cut. This narrows D32 and D75. **(6) Region drag is Alt, and Alt is not Subtract.** The grid-cell region selection is now **Alt+drag** (Ctrl+drag no longer starts it; this supersedes that clause of D75 and the D71/D72 Ctrl+drag wording). While the Fieldier uses Alt for the region gesture, **Alt no longer means Subtract for the selection tools**; Subtract remains the right mouse button, and the mask shape algebra retains its `Subtract` mode. The Wand's own Alt contiguity override is unchanged. **NOTE:** this deliberate divergence (Alt = region gesture for Rectangle/Lasso, Alt = contiguity override for the Wand, and Alt is not Subtract) is called out explicitly here, mirroring how D75 already documents the Wand's divergence. Only the clauses named above change: D50, D71, D72 and D75 are otherwise intact, and every existing decision row is preserved. | ✅ locked |
| **D77** | **Fieldier selection behavior refinements (2026-09-26).** Four refinements to how the Fieldier moves and combines a selection and how its buttons behave, replacing the corresponding clauses of D76 and the line-segment region wording of D71/D75. **(1) A selection move never moves pixels.** Dragging the selection AREA (`AreaMove`) moves ONLY the selection (its rect + mask); it must NOT cut/paste pixels and must NOT push an undo step. Moving pixels is exclusively the transform object's job (D32). The live preview shows the translated mask boundary, so the mask's notches/protrusions stay visible during the drag. This re-affirms D50/D70 ("a drag from inside a selection moves the selection AREA only and never mutates pixels") and **supersedes D76 item 2's clause** that made `move_selection_to_command` the `AreaMove` commit: the mask-aware `move_selection_to_command` remains available in core for other callers, but `AreaMove` does not use it. **(2) The existing selection stays visible while combining.** While a Shift-add / subtract gesture is in progress, the pre-gesture base selection keeps its committed marching-ants on screen while the new area is swept, so the user can see the current boundaries while extending or subtracting. The live region overlay paints the base selection's ants underneath the new cells. **(3) The right button never deselects or starts a new selection.** A right-button drag subtracts the swept area from the current selection. With no current selection, the right button is a no-op (it must not clear or create a selection). Subtract uses the same swept-area rules as the left button, just with `SelectMode::Subtract`. This supersedes any clause implying a right-drag starts a fresh marquee and then subtracts it. **(4) Alt region selection is a freehand "sweep", and its button chooses add/subtract.** The grid-cell region gesture is **Alt+left = add** every grid cell the pointer passes over and **Alt+right = remove** every grid cell the pointer passes over. It is no longer limited to the cells along the straight line between press and current position: all cells the pointer traverses during the drag are collected (deduped, path-ordered). This supersedes D71/D75/D76's line-segment region wording; `tile_cells_on_segment` remains the per-step collector, but the gesture accumulates across the whole drag. Only the clauses named above change: D50, D70, D71, D72, D75 and D76 are otherwise intact, and every existing decision row is preserved. | ✅ locked |

This supersedes the release-time mode derivation implicit in D77 item 3; the right-button rule itself (never deselect, never create a selection, no-op with none) still stands and is re-affirmed. **(2) Every Fieldier child subtracts on the right button, symmetrically with the left.** For ALL children (Rectangle marquee, Wand, Lasso, Alt region sweep), the right button applies the SAME selection shape the left button would, combined with `SelectMode::Subtract`; the left button combines with `Add` when Shift is held and `Replace` otherwise. Add : Replace`; a secondary click applies with `Subtract`. The pre-gesture selection is passed explicitly as the merge base. ## Selection Policy Addendum

These are correctness invariants for the D68–D72 and D75–D79 selection work. They are recorded here as well as in code because each one has already been violated at least once during implementation.

- **`union_shape` must always scan its cells.** Two disjoint rectangles do **not** fill their combined bounding box, so the "both are rectangular → return an all-true mask" shortcut is wrong. An early version shipped it and the existing tests caught it. Unlike a drag, a union happens once per gesture, so the scan is not a hot path. Do not "optimise" this.
- **A rectangular selection move is O(1).** `translated_to` early-returns `capture` for the rectangular form, so dragging a large rectangle allocates no mask. The mask form still materialises, and the earlier `translated_shape` + `capture_mask` pair on the release path was O(area) per release; that is why the release path calls `translated_to`.
- **The canvas widget raises `stroke_started` on the first drag _delta_, not on the press.** That is why `handle_select_interactions` only treats it as a press while `gesture.is_idle()` — otherwise the first movement of a live gesture re-anchors its origin one frame after the press set it.
- **A double mirror restores the source, not the mirror.** `mirrored_shape` applied twice returns the original mask. Tests that assert `once == twice` are wrong, not the code.
- **The `Ownership::ALL` manifest is a guardrail, not documentation.** `ownership_source_inventory_matches_session_and_host_boundaries` compares it against the real `ProjectSession` field set; adding a field without updating the manifest fails the suite.
- **A selection tool switch goes through `ToolbarEvent::ToolSelected`,** which commits any in-flight transform session first, so the document is never left mid-transform.
